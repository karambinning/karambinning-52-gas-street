I · The Site

A canal-fronted plot, eight-and-a-half metres wide, opposite the Grade II Toll House.

The plot at 52 Gas Street has been a brownfield gap on Birmingham's historic canal district for years — a sliver of land too narrow for almost any conventional development. Its scale, and the listed Toll House beside it, are not constraints. They are the brief.

The scheme restores and repurposes the Grade II-listed Gas Street Toll House into new living space, and threads a new public staircase down from Gas Street to the canal towpath — reconnecting two levels of the city that the twentieth century forgot.

II · The Building

A super-slender tower, clad entirely in solar.

The narrow footprint unlocks something unprecedented — a fully integrated Building-Integrated Photovoltaic façade running the full height of the southwest elevation, with no obstructing massing to shade it. The form is the energy strategy.

Twenty-one storeys of co-living, shared lounges, coworking and rooftop terraces stack inside the eight-and-a-half-metre line. The building is read as a single architectural object — slim, vertical, and unmistakably of its moment.

III · The Energy

More electricity out than in. The surplus, shared.

The southwest face is the engine. Sunlight strikes a continuous photovoltaic skin and turns the elevation itself into a generator — enough that the building runs net-positive across the year, producing more than its residents consume.

The surplus is routed into a proposed local microgrid, distributing clean power to neighbouring buildings — and, in time, potentially lighting Gas Street itself once again. The street that took its name from gaslight, lit by solar.

IV · The Living

Co-living for the city's next generation.

The interior is sized for Birmingham's growing population of young professionals — single units, shared facilities, and a building that works as a vertical neighbourhood instead of a stack of strangers.

Currently in planning.
